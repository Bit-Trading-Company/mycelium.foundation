Grown, not built

Introducing the Mycelium Foundation, and the network it exists to support.

The Mycelium Foundation is a nonprofit. It exists to support the Mycelium Network: a decentralized network designed to be fast, inexpensive and scalable, and to stay simple as it grows.

That sentence is easy to write and hard to live up to. Most networks that set out to be fast, cheap and decentralized end up choosing two. We think the third doesn’t have to be given up, and we think the clue to how is underfoot.

The oldest network on the forest floor

Beneath almost every forest runs a mycelium: the branching body of fungi, threading through soil in every direction at once. It finds food, trades with plant roots, routes around damage, and has no single point that can fail. Nothing directs it. Each hyphal tip follows a few local rules, and the colony’s intelligence comes from everyone following them at once.

When researchers measured how a woodland fungus builds its network, they found something remarkable: as it grew, it became more efficient and more robust at the same time, while its relative cost fell. It did this by reinforcing the paths that carried flow and recycling the ones that didn’t. That is the property we want in a ledger.

What the Foundation does

Four kinds of work: open research, grants for the teams building on the network, stewardship of a clear and neutral specification, and education. You can read more about each on our about page.

Just as important is what we won’t do. We don’t own the network and we won’t control it. We don’t sell tokens or predict prices. We fund work, not winners. And we’ll measure our success by how little the network needs us.

Where things stand

The network is in research. The testnet is in preparation, and the litepaper is being written. We’ve published a field guide to the design direction, so the ideas can be examined in the open before the details are fixed.

If any of this is interesting to you, the best thing you can do is start at the edge: read, ask questions, and tell us what you’d build. A mycelium grows at its tips. So does a network.
